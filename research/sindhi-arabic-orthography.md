## 1. Sindhi-Arabic Orthography & Extended Character Set (52 Letters)
Standard Arabic uses 28 letters; Sindhi uses 52 distinct characters modified with specific diacritics and dot placements. Forensic transcription must distinguish subtle dot counts and orientation:

- **Four-dot letters**: 
  - ٽ (ṭe with four dots arranged in a square or diamond above)
  - ٺ (ṭhe with four dots arranged vertically/horizontally)
  - ٿ (the with four dots arranged vertically or in a quad pattern)
  - ڄ (ǰim with four dots inside the curve)
  - ڃ (ñim with two dots vertically or quad in handwritten forms)
- **Special Implosive Consonants**:
  - ٻ (ɓe - implosive b, with two dots below placed horizontally)
  - ڄ (ʄe - implosive j, with two dots or special dot structure)
  - ڳ (ɠe - implosive g, with two bars or specific dots)
  - ڱ (ŋe - velar nasal, gaaf with dots)
  - ڏ (ɗe - implosive d, dal with dot below or above)
  - ڻ (ṇe - retroflex nasal n, nun with small tah / four dots)
- **Vowel and Base Diacritics**:
  - Zer (ِ), Zabar (َ), Pesh (ُ), Jazm (ْ), Tashdeed (ّ) commonly omitted in exam questions except to clarify ambiguous technical terminology or poetry questions.
  - Alif Madda (آ) at word initials.

## 2. Urdu Nastaliq vs. Sindhi Naskh Typographic Conventions
- **Sindhi Papers**: Predominantly written in **Naskh** script (horizontal baseline, clear separated word boundaries, distinct dots).
- **Urdu Papers**: Often set or written in **Nastaliq** (cascading baseline from top-right to bottom-left, ligatures stacked vertically, overlapping letter forms).
- **Handwritten Exam Cursive**:
  - Fast handwriting merges letter sequences: e.g., 'سین' (Seen) written as a continuous horizontal stroke without distinct teeth ('shoshas').
  - Nun-Ghunna (ں) versus standard Nun (ن) in final positions.
  - Choti Yeh (ی) and Badi Yeh (ے) distinctions: Badi Yeh slopes back to the right in Urdu/Sindhi, whereas Choti Yeh curves upward to the left.

## 3. Examination Question Paper Structure & Patterns
Examination materials across Sindh Education Boards (BIEK, BSEK, BISE Hyderabad, Sukkur, Larkana, Mirpurkhas) follow rigorous hierarchical structures:

1. **Header Block**:
   - Board or School Name (e.g., "Board of Intermediate & Secondary Education", "Sindh Secondary Certificate Examination")
   - Subject (e.g., "مضمون: سنڌي لازمی", "اسلاميات", "General Science", "English Compulsory")
   - Class / Grade (e.g., "Class IX", "دہم", "نهم")
   - Time Allowed & Total Marks (e.g., "Time: 3 Hours", "ڪل نمبر: 75", "کل نشانات: 100")

2. **Section Hierarchy**:
   - Section A / حصہ اول / حصو الف: Multiple Choice Questions (ڪثير الانتخابي سوالات)
   - Section B / حصہ دوم / حصو ب: Short Answer Questions (مختصر سوالات جا جواب)
   - Section C / حصہ سوم / حصو ج: Descriptive / Detailed Questions (تفصيلي سوالات جا جواب)

3. **Numbering Conventions**:
   - Western Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3...) or Eastern Arabic numerals (۱, ۲, ۳, ۴, ۵, ۶, ۷, ۸, ۹)
   - Question headers: "سوال نمبر ۱:", "Q. No. 1:", "س ۱:"
   - Marks notation: "(Marks: 5)", "(5 نمبر)", "(نمبر: 10)" aligned to the outer margin (left margin in RTL documents).

4. **Mixed LTR and RTL Content**:
   - Science and Mathematics papers often feature Urdu/Sindhi instruction lines (RTL) followed by English equations, formulas, or biological nomenclature (LTR).
   - Each paragraph and table cell must carry its independent bidi direction (`rtl` or `ltr`).
